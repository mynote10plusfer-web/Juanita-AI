# Cultural research seed — Paisa / Medellín and Canarian / Tenerife

**Status:** research notes for Phase C2 design; NOT yet an approved in-app dossier.  
**Research date:** 2026-11-10.  
**Use:** build a cited, user-reviewable cultural layer. These sources must not be converted into personality stereotypes.

## Colombia — Antioqueño / Paisa, Medellín

### Supported observations

- A 2023 peer-reviewed study by researchers affiliated with Universidad de Antioquia examines the vitality of *voseo* in Medellín. It notes coexistence of **vos, tú and usted** in Antioquia and documents the social meaning and continuing use of voseo in Medellín. Therefore, a Paisa character should not mechanically replace every second-person form with *vos*: selection depends on speaker, relationship, context and register.
  - Source: Ji Son Jang & Ana Isabel García Tesoro, “El voseo como orgullo paisa: el uso pronominal en el español hablado en Medellín (Colombia)”, *Boletín de Filología*, Universidad de Chile, 2023.
  - URL: https://www.revistapuntogenero.uchile.cl/index.php/BDF/article/view/73296
  - Related full-text landing page: https://boletinfilologia.uchile.cl/index.php/BDF/article/view/73296/75913
  - Scope: Medellín; pronominal address; empirical study using surveys and interviews.
  - Confidence: high for the study's stated findings; not a universal rule for every Paisa speaker.

- The Universidad de Antioquia's public explanation of *parcero* traces it through research on *parlache* and describes how some vocabulary spread beyond its original social groups. This is a warning against treating *parlache* as the whole Paisa dialect or making the character constantly use street slang.
  - Source: Universidad de Antioquia, “El parcero del español”.
  - URL: https://www.udea.edu.co/wps/portal/udea/web/inicio/udea-noticias/udea-noticia/contenido/asnoticias/cultura/parcero-del-espanol/
  - Scope: lexical history and diffusion of *parlache*.
  - Confidence: high for the university's account; individual word frequency and appropriateness remain context-dependent.

- A lexicographic research article hosted by Universidad Nacional de Colombia describes *parlache* as a sociolect with particular social and generational origins. Its vocabulary cannot safely be used as a neutral synonym for ordinary contemporary speech in Medellín.
  - Source: Luz Stella Castañeda Naranjo, “El parlache: resultados de una investigación lexicográfica”, *Forma y Función*, 2005.
  - URL: https://revistas.unal.edu.co/index.php/formayfuncion/article/view/17977
  - Scope: parlache lexicography and sociolinguistic context.
  - Confidence: high for the academic description; the article is historical and should be supplemented before claiming current frequency.

### Character-engine use

- Model pronoun choice as a contextual language feature, not a fixed catchphrase.
- Offer natural Antioquian/Paisa vocabulary sparingly and only when the register fits; explain or avoid slang that may be vulgar, age-marked, stigmatized or group-specific.
- Keep the individual's humour, warmth, directness, sociability and values in the separate character profile.
- Do not infer religion, politics, morality, gender roles, relationship behavior, ambition, temperament or work ethic from Paisa origin.

## Spain — Canary Islands / Tenerife

### Supported observations

- The Academia Canaria de la Lengua describes Canarian Spanish as a differentiated variety with phonetic, grammatical and lexical traits, including seseo, aspiration of syllable-final /s/, generally limited use of *vosotros* and distinctive vocabulary. It also stresses internal diversity across the islands.
  - Source: Academia Canaria de la Lengua, “¿El español que se habla en Canarias es un dialecto o un habla?” (2024).
  - URL: https://www.academiacanarialengua.org/consultas/2024/02/dialecto-habla-canaria/
  - Scope: general Canarian Spanish; not a complete description of Tenerife alone.
  - Confidence: high for the Academy's general description.

- The Academy explicitly notes that the archipelago has shared broad features but island-specific variation, especially in vocabulary and some phonetic details. Its guidance says not to collapse the islands into one identical speech pattern.
  - Source: Academia Canaria de la Lengua, “¿Por qué no todas las islas canarias hablan de la misma manera?” (2023).
  - URL: https://www.academiacanarialengua.org/consultas/2023/03/particularidades-insulares/
  - Scope: inter-island variation.
  - Confidence: high for the Academy's summary.

- The Academy's *Diccionario básico de canarismos* explains that entries are geographically labelled and that a word recorded on Tenerife does not necessarily occur throughout the whole island. It includes common archipelago-wide words, island-specific words, phraseology and specialist vocabulary.
  - Source: Academia Canaria de la Lengua, “Características — Diccionario básico de canarismos”.
  - URL: https://www.academiacanarialengua.org/diccionario/caracteristicas/
  - Scope: lexicographic method and geographic labels.
  - Confidence: high; this should be the primary lexical reference for Tenerife entries.

- The Academy's 2024 consultation on island varieties also states that broad Canarian traits are shared by most speakers while local differences exist. It recommends specialist bibliography for a deeper description of individual island speech.
  - Source: Academia Canaria de la Lengua, “Sobre características de las variedades insulares” (2024).
  - URL: https://www.academiacanarialengua.org/consultas/2024/04/sobre-caracteristicas-variedades-insulares/
  - Scope: general and island-level variation.
  - Confidence: high for the stated overview.

### Character-engine use

- Use Canarian language guidance at the archipelago level only where sources support it; use Tenerife-specific words only when the Academy marks them for Tenerife.
- Do not force phonetic misspellings into text. In voice, a prompt cannot guarantee a native Tenerife accent; the actual selected voice must be evaluated.
- Do not use *guagua*, *gofio* or other recognizable terms as decorative catchphrases. Use them naturally and only where relevant.
- Avoid treating Tenerife, Gran Canaria, La Palma, La Gomera, El Hierro, Lanzarote and Fuerteventura as interchangeable.
- Keep the individual's stable character separate from the regional language layer.

## Cross-profile safeguards

- Cultural evidence describes language, references, history and practices; it does not define an individual's personality.
- Each source claim must retain source URL, publisher, retrieval date, topic, geographic scope and confidence.
- Distinguish academic evidence, institutional guidance, local testimony and model interpretation.
- If sources disagree or a claim is weak, show uncertainty and keep it out of the active prompt until reviewed.
- A user-approved dossier is a contextual reference, not an instruction to imitate every stereotype or speak in slang constantly.
- The dossier must remain inspectable, editable, revocable and cached for offline use.
